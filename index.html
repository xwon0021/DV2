Australian
Made Products

A data visualisation by Wong Xin Thung · 9 October 2026

Behind Australian-made products are local businesses, specialised industries and a changing workforce. Where are they concentrated, and how does their contribution reach beyond the factory?

Manufacturing data provide context for Australian-made products; they do not identify every locally made product or establish Australian Made certification.

01

Place and concentration

Where manufacturing happens

A strong manufacturing presence can mean two different things:
many businesses, or many businesses relative to the local population.
Start with that distinction before looking at individual facilities.

The maps use Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) local areas
called Statistical Areas Level 2 (SA2s). The first compares businesses
per 10,000 residents; the second shows business counts.
Together, they distinguish relative concentration from cluster size.

⚠️ Critical Denominator Context

Canning Vale Commercial (WA) recorded 156 manufacturing businesses with an ERP of just 5 residents (312,000 per 10k residents). A tiny residential denominator creates an extreme rate — it reflects a dedicated industrial precinct, not 312,000 factories.

High concentration does not always mean a large business cluster.

RECENT PERFORMANCE

Growth, scale and sector profile do not move together

V6

Sales growth and value-added growth

Year-on-year percentage change across manufacturing subdivisions, 2023–24 to 2024–25.

Key Finding · Income vs Value Added Divergence

Primary metals sales surged +23.8% while IVA fell -3.1% in 2024–25 (a 26.9 percentage point divergence). This highlights how raw turnover and retained industry value added can diverge under volatile commodity pricing.

Sales growth is nominal and includes price changes. IVA reflects net economic contribution rather than gross revenue or profit.

While growth rates capture short-term changes, comparing employment, wages, sales and value added reveals distinct structural profiles across sectors.

03

Change over time

How manufacturing has changed

Manufacturing employment recovered from its 2019–20 low of 830.2 thousand to 900.8 thousand in 2024–25. It remained below the 1,005.5 thousand recorded in 2006–07.

The recovery did not restore the starting national level. Follow the total first, then track individual industries to see how their employment and rankings changed.

04

Beyond the factory

Australian-made in context

Manufacturing also supplies the rest of the economy. Construction is its largest outward relationship shown here, receiving A$74.215bn in domestic intermediate use in 2023–24.

These domestic connections are one part of the story. Recent industry changes, overseas markets and attitudes to buying local reveal different aspects of Australian-made products.

V11

Industry value added: 2023–24 versus 2024–25

Food IVA increased by A$2.348bn; textiles and clothing fell by A$336m. Follow each subdivision between the two years on a shared value scale.

Key Takeaway · Industry Value Added Trajectory

Food manufacturing gained +A$2,348m in IVA (reaching A$31,279m) between 2023–24 and 2024–25, continuing to outpace all other sectors. In sharp contrast, Textiles, Leather, Clothing & Footwear contracted by -A$336m (falling to A$2,216m), illustrating ongoing sectoral divergence.

Domestic relationships connect industries; recorded exports connect origins and overseas markets.

84% of Australians report being more likely to buy when they know a product is Australian Made. (Reflects stated consumer preference and brand affinity; not audited register of purchase behavior).

Australian-made products sit within an uneven manufacturing landscape:
different local clusters, industry strengths and employment paths.
Interest in buying local adds a consumer perspective,
while actual purchases remain a separate question.

Data and sources
